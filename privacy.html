PRIVACY POLICY

Privacy

Effective November 25, 2026.

Zignal is a private messenger that sends messages inside shielded Zcash transactions. There is no Zignal server and no Zignal account. We, the developer of Zignal, don’t collect, receive or store anything you type, send or keep in the app. Zignal is experimental and has not been audited.

This page covers the Zignal iPhone app, the Zignal demo website, and Apple’s TestFlight, which delivers the app to testers.

In short

Account, phone number or emailNone needed
Analytics, tracking, adsNone
Logs or crash reporting by ZignalNone (Apple’s TestFlight sends crash reports; see below)
Where your data livesOn your phone, encrypted
What we can hand over if askedNothing from the app: we never have it
What the light server can learnWhich transactions you read, not what’s in them (see below)

The app

What stays on your phone

  • Your profiles, contacts, chats, keys and wallet. They’re encrypted on the phone and kept out of iCloud and device backups.
  • Your PINs. They open a vault kept in the Keychain for this phone only and sealed by the iPhone’s Secure Enclave, so a copy taken off the phone can’t be opened.
  • Your 24-word recovery phrase. It is shown on the phone and never sent anywhere.

What leaves your phone

  • Messages: each one goes out as an encrypted memo inside a shielded Zcash transaction. It is stored on the Zcash blockchain permanently and can’t be deleted by anyone. Sender, recipient, amount and the message are hidden on chain. Your contact can read what you send them.
  • Wallet traffic: the app talks only to the light server at the top of your server list (Network settings). The default servers are public and run by others, not by Zignal: zec.rocks, Stardust and Cake Wallet. You can use your own instead.

What the server sees

  • Which transactions you read: to read a message, the app asks the server for that transaction by its ID, and it checks the messages you send the same way until they are mined. So the server learns which transactions carry messages for you, with Tor on or off. It can’t read them: the messages stay encrypted. We are working on hiding this.
  • Tor off: the server also sees your IP address with every request: transactions you send, the ones you read, and blocks you download. Together with the transaction IDs, that shows who reads which message and, if the same server saw it sent, who wrote to whom.
  • Tor on (the default for new profiles): sending and reading go through Tor, so the server sees a Tor exit instead of your IP address. Block downloads still connect directly, and so does the wallet’s live feed of new transactions (the ones not mined yet, the same for every wallet), which stays open while the app is unlocked so that a message shows up within seconds. So the server learns that you use Zcash and when the app is open, and it may link the transactions you read to that by their timing. The Tor network’s entry relay sees your IP address but not what you send.
  • What helps today: keep Tor on (Network settings), add a VPN with a kill switch, or use a light server you run yourself.
  • The app’s README explains this in detail, including how a VPN helps: network privacy ↗.

Phone permissions

  • Camera: only to scan a contact’s invite QR code on the New chat screen. Apple’s scanner reads the code on the phone; no picture is saved or sent. You can paste the invite instead.
  • Pasteboard: what you copy in Zignal stays on this phone (no Universal Clipboard) and expires after 60 seconds. Locking the app removes it sooner.
  • Notifications: only if you turn on a reminder: every day at a time you pick, or after a length of time you pick, once or again and again. Your iPhone makes it; it always says the same words and never whether anything arrived. No push service and no server are involved, and Zignal still checks nothing while it’s locked.
  • Zignal doesn’t ask for your contacts, photos, location or microphone.

The demo website

  • The demo is a static page that simulates the app. It sets no cookies, runs no analytics, loads nothing from other sites, and keeps nothing you type after a reload. It never asks for real PINs, recovery phrases or personal details.
  • It is hosted on GitHub Pages. GitHub logs every visitor’s IP address for security; see GitHub Pages ↗ and GitHub’s privacy statement ↗.

TestFlight

Testers get Zignal through Apple’s TestFlight. Apple, not Zignal, runs it and shares some data with us as the developer:

  • for testers we invite by email: the name and email address used for the invitation (not shown for testers who join through a public link);
  • your device model, iOS version and language, how many sessions and crashes the app had, and crash logs. Apple doesn’t let testers turn this off;
  • feedback and screenshots, only if you choose to send them, with some device details (carrier, time zone, connection type, battery and free space).

We use this only to fix and improve Zignal, and Apple doesn’t allow us to share it with anyone else. A crash log shows where in the app’s code a crash happened; Zignal writes no logs of its own. See Apple’s TestFlight & Privacy ↗ page for what Apple collects.

Children

Zignal is meant for people 13 and older. Where you live may set a higher age, for messaging apps or for holding cryptocurrency. We don’t knowingly collect data from anyone, children included: the app sends us nothing.

If you contact us

If you email zkchat@tuta.com or report an issue on GitHub, we receive your address or GitHub account and what you write. We use it only to reply and fix problems.
